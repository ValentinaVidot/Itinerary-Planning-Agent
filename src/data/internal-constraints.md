# Pilot constraints and decision assumptions

SYNTHETIC CLASSROOM DATA — fixture v1, 2026-09-17.

The earliest pilot start is six weeks away. Two engineers and one designer can
work on one substantial improvement before then. A part-time support colleague
can devote a total of four hours per week to pilot setup and assistance. Treat
these as constraints, not as proof that any particular change can be completed.

The team can spend up to SGD 8,000 on pilot preparation and support. This is a
fictional company budget, separate from the student's USD 1 model/search budget.
The pilot should involve at most five organizations and run for four weeks.
The strategy lead prefers a narrow learning objective over a broad public launch.
No contract has been signed and no customer data has been collected.

Candidate improvements include a CSV import preview with actionable errors,
a self-service onboarding checklist, one external integration, or finer-grained
permissions. There are no validated delivery estimates for these options. Do not
invent exact implementation costs or pretend the team can deliver all of them.

A proposed pilot should specify what to learn and how success would be assessed.
Possible measures include setup time, support requests, and whether participants
can trace a reported theme back to source feedback. These are proposed measures,
not measured results. The recommendation must also identify missing evidence
before any broader launch decision.
